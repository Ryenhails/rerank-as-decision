The position

A general vision-language model can serve as a multimodal reranker through System One decisions, and decision tuning can improve its reranking.

Motivation

Why the position matters.

DeploymentIf the VLM that answers can also rerank, there is no separate reranker to train, serve and keep up to date.

ResearchIf reranking is one decision among many, it can improve with decision tuning across tasks, without reranking-specific training.

Method

Two ways to ask. Same instruction, query and candidates.

Choice readout

“Which candidate best matches the query, following the instruction?”

Yes/no readout

“Does this candidate match the query, following the instruction?”

Schematic; bar heights are illustrative. The yes/no readout is the form of supervised pointwise rerankers.

See the exact prompt

The choice prompt, simplified. Thinking is off; the next-token logits restricted to A–P give one probability per candidate.

Alternative views and tests

What challenges the position, and what would tell.

Alternative views

  • Stronger VLMs make stronger dedicated rerankers tooScale and model generation differ; the same-backbone comparison is open.
  • A general VLM is too expensiveMore than 3× the parameters and 1.1–1.5× the latency of Reranker-8B; no extra model if the VLM is already served.
  • Small models cannot do itGemma 4 E2B and E4B lower the first stage.
  • Dedicated listwise rerankers already existZipRerank trains a reranker with the same readout; the two routes can coexist.
  • The readout has practical limitsAt most 16 candidates per prompt; option order and label preferences matter.

Testing the position

  • Multimodal decision tuningRefuted if it does not improve reranking, above all on natural images and image-only queries.
  • The gap on one backboneZero-shot vs. decision-tuned vs. dedicated, one VLM at several sizes.
  • Backbone and image budgetQwen3.8-27B at Gemma 4's budget, or Gemma 4 at a larger one.
  • The choice readoutMeasure order sensitivity and calibration, and correct for order.

Read the abstract

Multimodal rerankers are usually dedicated models: a vision-language model (VLM) is trained on retrieval data to score the candidates of a query. Scoring options is not specific to reranking. System One decision models such as Jev map a state and a typed question to one probability per option without generating text, and general models can make such decisions with little or no training. Reranking is one such decision, with the query as the state and the candidates as the options. We therefore argue that a general VLM can serve as a multimodal reranker through System One decisions, and that decision tuning can improve its reranking. We ask a VLM one choice question over the top 16 candidates of a first-stage retriever and rank the candidates by the probabilities of their labels, read from one forward pass. On MMEB-V2 image retrieval, this choice scores 5.4 to 7.3 hit@1 above a yes/no question per candidate, and the yes/no question falls below the first stage. With the choice, zero-shot Qwen3.8-27B beats the supervised Qwen3-VL-Reranker-2B on all three benchmarks and comes within 1.6 points of Reranker-8B on each. It matches Reranker-8B on MMEB-V2 visual documents (84.79 vs. 84.78 nDCG@5), at 1.1 to 1.5 times its latency. JEV-27B-VL, the same backbone tuned on text decisions only, improves page reranking by 0.6 to 1.1 points, with gains on most tasks (sign test, p ≤ 0.017), and leaves natural images unchanged. The zero-shot model is larger than the supervised rerankers and of a newer generation, so these comparisons are confounded; we discuss this and other alternative views, and the experiments that would test the position.
